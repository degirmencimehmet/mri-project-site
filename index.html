Intelligent MRI Image Analysis and Automated Report Generation System

An AI-assisted decision-support prototype that analyzes spine MRI, highlights selected abnormalities and drafts a structured radiology report for the radiologist to review, edit and approve.

Read the reports Project proposal (PDF)

About the project

Magnetic Resonance Imaging is widely used to evaluate spinal disorders such as intervertebral disc herniation, disc degeneration and spinal stenosis. Reading an examination means reviewing several image sequences and anatomical structures, so reporting is time-consuming, especially in facilities with high imaging workloads.

This project develops an AI-assisted clinical decision-support system that analyzes spine MRI, identifies selected abnormalities and generates a structured draft report. The aim is to help radiologists interpret examinations more efficiently, reduce repetitive manual work and make reports more consistent.

The system supports radiologists; it does not replace them. It is built as a research prototype to evaluate the feasibility of AI-assisted spine MRI interpretation and automated reporting.

Target findings

Disc herniation
Displacement of disc material beyond the normal margins of the intervertebral space.
Disc degeneration
Loss of disc hydration and height, seen as reduced signal on T2-weighted images.
Spinal canal narrowing
Stenosis of the canal that may compress neural structures, with measurements where the models support them.

How it works

Deep learning models, including convolutional neural networks and segmentation architectures such as U-Net, will be investigated and trained on publicly available, annotated spine MRI datasets.

  1. Upload and preprocess

    MRI images are uploaded through the web interface and normalized for analysis.

  2. Detect and segment

    Models identify anatomical structures and abnormalities and extract measurable information.

  3. Draft the report

    Findings fill predefined medical templates, with a language model constrained to the detected findings where appropriate.

  4. Review and approve

    The radiologist examines highlighted findings, edits the draft and approves it before export.
